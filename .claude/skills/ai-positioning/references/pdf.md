## Operating Model

Follow this six-step process. Do not skip steps.

### Step 1 — Gather Two Critical Inputs

1. **The account being pitched** (e.g., Baker McKenzie, JPMorgan Chase, NHS England)
2. **The PDF scenario** — specific context including:
   - What PDF tool(s) the client currently uses (Adobe, Nitro, Foxit, free tools, none)
   - Approximate user count and mix (power users vs occasional users)
   - Current licensing model (subscription, perpetual, free)
   - Key pain points (cost, compliance, deployment, security, feature gaps)

If either is missing, ask before proceeding.

### Step 2 — Research the Account Externally

Use publicly available sources. Focus on:
- The client's **document workflow landscape** — create, share, sign, archive PDFs
- **Compliance and regulatory requirements** — redaction, data protection, audit trails, accessibility (Section 508, WCAG)
- **Remote and hybrid workforce** — desktop vs cloud needs, VDI/Citrix
- Technology stack — Microsoft 365, SharePoint, DMS
- Budget pressures and vendor consolidation initiatives

### Step 3 — Review Industry-Specific PDF Materials

Consider:
- Industry-specific angle (Legal, Financial Services, Healthcare, Government)
- Compliance-driven requirements (redaction, encryption, Bates numbering, e-signatures)
- Volume licensing opportunity

**Always lead with value** — cost savings, compliance assurance, deployment simplicity, user productivity.

### Step 4 — Identify and Apply the Right Positioning Angle

Major market reality: PDF is a commodity skill every knowledge worker needs, but enterprises are dramatically overpaying for it through Adobe subscription lock-in. The market is shifting toward cost-conscious, security-first alternatives.

**Scenario A — Client is locked into an Adobe Enterprise Agreement:**
- Position Power PDF as the **cost-saving alternative** that eliminates subscription dependency
- Perpetual licensing — pay once, own forever
- Same enterprise features (redaction, Bates numbering, OCR, e-sign, batch) at up to 60% less
- Desktop-first — data stays local, no mandatory cloud
- Message: "You're paying Adobe rent. Power PDF lets you own it — same features, 60% less, forever."

**Scenario B — Client is using free or basic PDF tools:**
- Position as the **professional upgrade** that unlocks enterprise capabilities
- Free tools lack redaction, encryption, Bates numbering, batch processing
- One compliance failure costs more than a lifetime of Power PDF licenses
- Message: "Free PDF tools are a ticking compliance bomb. Power PDF gives you enterprise control at a fraction of what Adobe charges."

### Step 5 — Build Differentiating Position Around Core Pillars

1. **Cost savings** — Perpetual vs Adobe subscription. Power PDF Advanced at $179 one-time vs Adobe Acrobat Pro at $276/year = break-even in under 8 months, 78% savings over 3 years.
2. **Security** — Desktop-first; documents processed locally by default. No mandatory cloud, no third-party servers touching sensitive content. Critical for regulated industries, government, data sovereignty.
3. **Ease of use** — Familiar Office-style ribbon. Users productive from day one. Minimal training. Unlike Adobe's complex, feature-buried UI.
4. **Enterprise deployment** — Full Citrix and Windows Server support, App-V, MSI/GPO, volume licensing with centralised management. Built for enterprise IT — not retrofitted from a consumer product.

### Step 6 — Produce Output

- Executive-quality positioning brief — structured, value-led, account-specific
- Lead with value-based outcomes — what the customer saves, secures, simplifies
- Structure for stated audience (CIO, IT Director, Procurement, LOB)
